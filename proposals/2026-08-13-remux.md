# Remux - repackaging a film so a browser and an iPhone will open it

**Status**: Draft 2026-08-13. Awaiting approval.

**Goal**: Serve a film in a container the client will actually open, without re-encoding
the picture, and with seeking that works - so a real library stops being one tenth
watchable on iOS and in a browser.

**Tier**: **T3.** It changes what `media.stream` means. Today the bytes are the file and
`offset` addresses it directly; a remuxed stream's bytes do not exist until we make them,
so byte offsets stop being a stable address and seeking needs a different mechanism. That
is a wire-contract change on the one method where a mistake hands out the library rather
than an error message. It also adds host-side generated bytes, which is a new class of
thing this host has never had.

**Depends on**: nothing new. `@peerloom/host` phases 1 and 2 are in, both adapters work,
and ffmpeg is already in the image (the folder adapter cannot scan without ffprobe, and
Debian ships them together).

---

## Why this is the release and not an optimisation

The measurement is in DECISIONS 2026-08-12, taken over Tim's whole 12,197-file drive.
Restated only as far as this proposal needs it, and **not to be re-derived**:

| | Films (240) | TV (2,746) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| matroska | 220 (92%) | 2,262 (82%) |
| mov / mp4 | 20 (8%) | 266 (10%) |
| avi | - | 218 (8%) |

**83% of the media library is in a container an iPhone will not open**, and Chrome and
Safari refuse the same container for the same reason. Two independent engines, one
refusal. The web player shipped in PR #10 now demonstrates this daily: it counts, per
list, how many files the browser will play, and on a normal collection that number is
about a tenth.

The repair ladder, cheapest first:

- **Container rewrite only**: 190 films and 1,635 episodes, **~61% of the library**. The
  streams inside are already what an iPhone and a browser want (H.264 or HEVC video, AAC
  audio). Nothing is re-encoded.
- **Container rewrite plus AUDIO re-encode**: ~650 files, dominated by HEVC + AC-3
  television. Audio is a rounding error against video to encode. Possibly fewer, since
  Apple does support Dolby Digital in MP4 - the client settles it.
- **Full video encode**: the 218 AVI / MPEG-4 Part 2 files, **7%**, all in TV Shows.

**This proposal covers the first two rungs only.** The third is a different machine
(it needs a real encoder and hardware acceleration) and is deliberately out.

**Plex is the existence proof** (Tim, 2026-08-13). Its web player opens his MKVs because
the server rewraps them on the way out - "Direct Stream" in Plex's vocabulary is exactly
rung one, and it falls back to a real transcode only for rung three. The shape of this
work is therefore settled by a shipped product rather than by argument.

## What remux is, mechanically

```
ffmpeg -i film.mkv -c:v copy -c:a copy -f <fragmented mp4 or hls> ...
```

`-c copy` is the whole point: the compressed video and audio are lifted out of one
container and written into another, byte for byte. No decoding, no encoding, no quality
loss. It is **I/O bound rather than CPU bound**, which is the opposite of transcode and
the reason a Pi-class box can do several at once.

Rung two adds `-c:a aac -b:a 192k` for the audio stream only, which even a weak CPU does
in a small fraction of real time.

---

## The hard part is seeking, and it decides the design

A remuxed stream is generated. Its byte offsets do not exist until ffmpeg produces them,
so "give me byte 2,400,000,000" is unanswerable without producing everything before it.
This is the same trap the Jellyfin adapter already avoids by demanding `static=true`
(DONE 2026-08-12): *a transcoded stream has no stable byte offsets to seek to, because
those bytes do not exist until the server makes them.*

Three ways out, and the choice is not close.

**A. Progressive, `accept-ranges: none`.** What PearTune's worklet shim does for its
transcode path today (`worklet/shim.js`). Rejected outright, and it is already named as
unacceptable in this repo's CLAUDE.md: nobody scrubs a song, everybody scrubs a film.

**B. Restart-at-seek.** Kill ffmpeg, restart with `-ss <time>`, serve a fresh stream. Cheap
to build and genuinely used in the wild. The problem is that a plain `<video>` element and
AVPlayer both seek by BYTE RANGE over HTTP, not by time - so this needs a byte-to-time
mapping we would have to fake from an average bitrate. That is approximate on constant
bitrate and wrong on variable, which is most video.

**C. HLS.** Segment the output; serve a playlist of segments and their durations. Seeking
becomes "which segment", which is exact and needs no bitrate guess. It is what Plex and
Jellyfin both do.

**Recommendation: C, HLS**, with fragmented-MP4 segments.

- **Native everywhere that matters.** Safari and iOS AVPlayer play HLS with no library at
  all. Android's ExoPlayer plays HLS natively, which matters because the phone client is
  the actual product.
- **Chrome and Firefox need `hls.js`**, a real dependency - but a build-time one in the
  dashboard bundle, which already exists and already carries its own JS. It does not enter
  the host image.
- **It is resumable and cancellable by construction.** A seek abandons segments rather
  than killing a process mid-write.

## The constraint that is easy to get backwards: remuxed output is the SAME SIZE

A transcode cache is smaller than its source - that is the point of transcoding. **A remux
cache is not.** Nothing is re-encoded, so a 4 GB film remuxes to about 4 GB.

That single fact rules out the obvious design. "Remux the film to a cache directory, then
serve it" would mean an Umbrel needing 4 GB of free system disk per film watched, on a box
whose library lives on an external drive precisely because the internal one is small.

**So: generate segments on demand, keep a small window, delete behind the playhead.**

- A rolling buffer of segments ahead of where the viewer is, measured in seconds rather
  than files. Target: **60 seconds ahead, a hard cap in the low hundreds of MB per
  session.**
- Segments behind the playhead are deleted. Seeking backwards regenerates them, which is
  cheap because remux is cheap.
- The cache lives in the DATA dir, never in the library - the library is mounted `:ro` by
  design and that is not negotiable.
- **A cap, enforced, with the session refused rather than the disk filled.** An Umbrel that
  fills its root filesystem is a bricked box, not a failed playback.

Sessions are per (item, client), reaped on disconnect and on a timeout. A revoked device's
session is torn down along with its connection - see Security below.

## What changes on the wire

Additive, and absent means today's behaviour.

- **`media.capabilities`** - the CLIENT declares what it can open: containers, video
  codecs, audio codecs, max resolution. Sent once per connection.
- **`media.stream` gains `mode`**: `direct` (today, unchanged) or `hls`.
- **`media.playlist`** - returns the HLS playlist for an item, with segment durations.
- **`media.segment`** - returns one segment's bytes. Byte-ranged like `media.stream`
  already is, because a segment IS a stable addressable object once produced.

**THE HOST DECIDES THE MODE, not the client.** The client says what it can play; the host
picks direct play whenever it will work, because direct play is free and correct. Letting
a client demand remux is how a box ends up remuxing files that would have played.

On the phone, the local HTTP shim (`worklet/shim.js` in PearTune) serves the playlist and
segments to the native player over `127.0.0.1`, pulling them across the P2P channel. That
shim already speaks HTTP and already parses Range, so this is a new route on an existing
server rather than a new server.

The web player calls the HTTP equivalents on the dashboard - and, per the rule PR #10
established, they must be **the same host-side session machinery the phone uses**, wrapped
in HTTP. One remux implementation, two transports. A second one is a second place for the
cache cap to be forgotten.

## Scope

**In:**

- Rung one: container rewrite, `-c copy`, MKV and AVI-with-friendly-streams into fMP4.
- Rung two: audio-only re-encode to AAC where the audio codec is the only blocker
  (DTS, TrueHD, and AC-3/E-AC-3 if the client says it cannot take them).
- HLS playlist and segments, on-demand, with a rolling window and a hard disk cap.
- Host-side mode decision from client capabilities.
- The web player using it, since that is where it can be verified today with no phone.
- Falling back to direct play whenever direct play works.

**Out, deliberately:**

- **Video re-encoding of any kind.** That is the 7% AVI bucket and it needs hardware
  acceleration, `/dev/dri` passed into the container and its own capacity work. Different
  proposal.
- Burning in PGS subtitles. That needs a video encoder, so it follows the same fate.
- Pre-generating anything. No background remux of a library, no warm cache.
- Bitrate adaptation. One rendition, the source's own. Adaptive bitrate needs re-encoding.
- Scrub-preview thumbnails. Plex generates these separately and they are cheap to mistake
  for part of this. Not here.

## Security

Remux is the first time this host **generates** bytes rather than reading them, and that
introduces two things it has never had: a child process, and writable scratch space.

- **The input path comes from the same id-to-path chokepoint as everything else**
  (`_resolveIn` in the folder adapter). ffmpeg is invoked with an argv array, never a
  shell string. A filename with a quote in it is normal in a real library.
- **The output path is ours**, minted per session in the data dir, never derived from
  anything a client sent.
- **A remux session is gated on a LIVE grant, checked the same way `media.stream` is**, and
  torn down when the grant goes. Revoke must kill the session and delete its segments, not
  merely stop new requests - a revoked device holding a playlist must not be able to keep
  pulling segments that are already on disk. This is the `cast.js` lesson in a new place:
  cutting the connection is not always cutting the access.
- **Every ffmpeg process is owned by a session and killed with it**, with a timeout for
  the case where a client vanishes without closing. An orphaned ffmpeg on a Pi is the whole
  box.

## Compat

- **Old peers**: none in the field. Still, the additive shape matters, because there will
  be. An unknown method returns a typed `NO_METHOD` and the channel survives, so a client
  that knows nothing of remux keeps direct-playing what it can.
- **No persisted schema change.** The segment cache is disposable by design - deleting it
  costs a re-remux and nothing else - so there is no migration and nothing to roll back.
- **The scan cache is untouched.** Remux decides at playback time from facts
  `host/probe.js` already records.

## Verify

1. `npm run verify` green in both repos.
2. **In the browser, against the real library.** The compatibility line PR #10 added is the
   measurement: today it reads about a tenth. After this it must read **the rung-one and
   rung-two buckets, roughly 83%** - and the files that still refuse must be the 218 AVI
   ones and no others.
3. **Seek into the middle of a two-hour MKV and have it land**, in the browser and on the
   phone. Then seek backwards. This is the acceptance test; a remux that only plays from
   the start is option A and was rejected.
4. **Watch the disk while it runs.** The cache must stay inside its cap for a full film,
   with the window advancing and old segments actually being deleted. Measured with a
   real film, not a clip.
5. **Revoke mid-remux and look at the screen.** The picture stops, the ffmpeg process is
   gone, and the segments are gone. Verifying the log line is not verifying the claim.
6. **Measure what it costs** on the Umbrel: concurrent remux sessions before it degrades.
   The audio-era numbers in `peartune/docs/transcode-capacity.md` say nothing about this
   and will be cited by somebody who misses the distinction.

## Rollback

- **A config flag, `remux: off`, returns the host to direct-play-only**, which is exactly
  today's behaviour. This is the rollback for the feature.
- **The image is pinned by digest** in `umbrel/docker-compose.yml`; reverting is re-pinning
  the previous digest, which is the rollback for the release.
- Nothing persisted needs undoing, because nothing persisted changes.

## Open questions

1. **Does AC-3 survive a plain rewrap into MP4?** Apple documents support for Dolby Digital
   and Dolby Digital Plus in MP4, which would move a large part of rung two into rung one
   and make ~650 files free instead of cheap. **Recommendation: find out with a real file
   on a real iPhone before building the audio path**, rather than planning around either
   answer. It is an afternoon and it changes what gets built.

2. **Where does the segment cache live on an Umbrel?** The data dir is on the internal
   disk, which is where the free space is not. **Recommendation: data dir anyway, with the
   cap set conservatively**, and an operator setting to point it elsewhere. Putting it on
   the media drive would mean writing to a volume mounted `:ro` on purpose, and changing
   that to serve a cache is a bad trade for a disposable file.

3. **Does the phone remux, ever?** Off-LAN, the host is doing the work and sending the
   result over an upload the household may not have. A phone that direct-played the MKV
   itself would send the same bytes and no more. **Recommendation: no.** Android already
   plays ~99% of this library directly, so remux on the phone would be work for iOS only -
   and iOS is where the CPU budget and the battery are worst. It is the wrong device.

None of these block approval. Question 1 has the largest effect on what gets built and
should be answered first, because it decides whether rung two is a path worth writing.
